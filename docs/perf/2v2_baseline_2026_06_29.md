# 2v2 Doubles Multi-Ply Lossless Baseline (2026-06-29)

Measured against the perf stack PR-A + PR-B + PR-C, with `lossy_damage_3bucket: false` (pure lossless). PR-D/E/F not yet merged into the base when this ran.

Reproducer: `cargo run --release -p vgc-solver --example measure_2v2`

## Headline finding

**Lossless multi-ply 2v2 solves are intractable at the current per-cell cost.** A single depth-1 root solve over the full doubles action matrix would take **~15 minutes wall-clock** even with PR-B's percent collapse applied. Multi-ply compounds this.

This isn't a "we need a faster step()" problem. It's a structural problem: the doubles joint-action space is ~32,000 cells per side-pair, each cell costs ~28ms to enumerate losslessly, and there's no clever bucketing left after PR-A/B that doesn't sacrifice HP fidelity.

## Measured numbers (§1 + §2)

| Scenario | P1 joints | P2 joints | Total cells | Per-cell wall (attack/attack) | Raw combos/cell | Outcomes/cell |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| OHKO neutral | 144 | 224 | 32,256 | 28.2 ms | 3,072 | 1,344 |
| Midgame 2HKO | 144 | 224 | 32,256 | 28.0 ms | 3,072 | 2,112 |
| Switch-heavy | 144 | 224 | 32,256 | 27.9 ms | 3,072 | 2,112 |

Extrapolated depth-1 lossless solve: 32,256 cells × 28 ms ≈ **~903 seconds (~15 min)** before any pruning.

§3-§6 (recursive solve numbers) didn't complete within the 5-minute test window because `wall_cap` is checked at recursion-node boundaries, not mid-cell — and a single cell-enumeration pass can exceed the cap on its own. Fixing the wall_cap granularity is a small follow-up; the per-cell number above is the actually-load-bearing measurement.

## Why so many cells?

Doubles legal_choices per side is approximately `(4 moves × 2 targets + 1 ally target + switches) × 2 slots` minus illegal pairs. The measurement clocks 144 P1 joints and 224 P2 joints — the asymmetry is from the bench mon availability differing per scenario builder. ~32k cells per side-pair.

## Why 28 ms per cell?

Each `enumerate_outcomes` call on an attack/attack joint:
- 4 attacks resolve (2 attackers × 2 sides).
- Each attack draws: accuracy (post-PR-B: 2 buckets), crit (2 buckets), damage roll (still 16 — PR-D not in base), secondary (post-PR-B: 2 buckets).
- Cross-product per attack: 2 × 2 × 16 × 2 = 128 combos.
- Four attacks: 128⁴ = 2.7 × 10⁸ combos, but most collapse via canonical-hash dedup, leaving ~3,072 raw combos / ~2,112 deduped outcomes per cell.
- The 28 ms is the cost of those ~3,072 `step()` calls.

PR-D (KO-split damage collapse) shipped after this baseline ran. For OHKO sites it collapses 16 → 1, taking the damage axis out entirely. For non-OHKO sites (which is the multi-turn "Midgame 2HKO" case the user actually cares about) it falls back to 16-bucket — no help. **This is the load-bearing limitation for the user's use case.**

## Bottleneck ranking (from the data + structure)

1. **Joint-action space size (32k cells).** Action-independence factoring (PR-I design doc) can mitigate this for "neutral" turns where actors don't cross-interact. Estimated win: ~6-15× aggregate per the PR-I doc.
2. **Per-cell damage-roll cost (16 buckets × non-OHKO).** Only fixable by either (a) shared-prefix continuations via the F4 outer-step refactor (PR-G), or (b) accepting lossy fidelity (PR-C 3-bucket — already shipped, opt-in). The F4 plan recommends shipping the no-op state-lifts (F4a/b) but **deferring F4c (the actual mid-loop yield) until the PR-D residual error is measured**.
3. **TT misses across multi-ply.** PR-J audit in flight; expected modest constant factor.

## Recommendation

The two design docs (`docs/design/pr-i-action-independence.md` and `docs/design/f4-outer-step-mid-loop-resume.md`) both call out the same critical missing measurement: **how much PR-D's KO-split + (optionally) PR-C's 3-bucket actually costs us in Nash value vs the exact 16-roll lossless enumeration**. This baseline doesn't answer that — it just establishes the lossless intractability.

Next concrete step is a small additional measurement: pick 10-20 representative depth-1 cells (small enough to solve lossless under wall_cap) and compare Nash value lossless vs Nash value with various lossy levels. That number gates whether the F4 / PR-G investment is worth it for the user's "non-last-turn, non-last-mon" goal.

If lossy 3-bucket damage gives ≤1% Nash value error across a corpus of mid-game 2v2 positions, **just use it** — the architectural investment isn't justified. If error is ≥5%, F4c becomes the priority.

## What didn't land in this PR

- §3-§6 numbers (recursive solves with full instrumentation). Needs wall_cap to be checked inside enumerate_outcomes' inner loop, or a fundamentally smaller scenario, or both.
- A lossless-vs-lossy Nash value diff. That's the next experiment.
- TT hit-rate baseline — folded into PR-J's audit instead.
